Where Americans move, and what it costs

Every year the Census Bureau asks a few million people where they lived a year ago. This report follows those movers across two decades of state-to-state migration data, paired with Zillow's home value index for every state they left and every state they landed in.

Prepared by Zoe Rowe for Financial Data Analytics

The gap between where people move from and where they land — and it has widened sharply since 2020.

Stop 1

1 Interstate migration has been remarkably stable for twenty years

Roughly 7.5 million people move from one U.S. state to another every year — about 2% of the population. The low point was 6.83 million in 2010, in the aftermath of the housing crash. The high point was 8.30 million in 2022, as remote work loosened the tie between where people lived and where they worked.

Total interstate movers by year

Stop 2

2 The South is absorbing the Northeast and Midwest's population

Add up net migration — people arriving minus people leaving — by region for every year since 2005, and the direction never changes. The South has gained a net 8.7 million people; the Northeast has lost 5.0 million and the Midwest 2.5 million. The West has been a net loser every year since 2021, shedding 153,000 people in 2024 alone.

Net migration by Census region, 2005–2024

Stop 3

3 Florida and Texas are the biggest winners; California and New York the biggest losers

Averaged over the five most recent years, Florida gained a net 665,000 people and Texas gained 532,000. On the other side, California lost a net 1.27 million people and New York lost 840,000 — roughly double what any other state lost.

Net migration, 5-year average

Stop 4

4 California to Texas is the single busiest route in the country

In 2024, an estimated 77,161 people moved from California to Texas — the largest flow between any two states, and nearly 30,000 more than the reverse trip.

Busiest state-to-state routes, 2024

Stop 5

5 Most movers are chasing lower housing costs

Weight every move by how many people made it, and 53% of movers in 2024 relocated to a state with a lower home value index than the one they left. That share has held between 52% and 55% every year since 2005.

Share of movers headed to a cheaper state

Stop 6

6 The price gap between origin and destination has nearly doubled since 2019

The movers-weighted home-value gap was −$10,895 in 2019. By 2021 it had jumped to −$24,899 as remote work let people leave expensive coastal metros for good, and it remains a wide −$18,321 in 2024.

Movers-weighted home value gap (destination − origin)

Stop 7

7 Home values surged hardest in the same states that are gaining people

Since 2005, home values are up 191% in Idaho, 178% in Utah, and 174% in Montana — three of the biggest net-migration winners on record. At the other end, Illinois (+37%) and Nevada (+41%) had the slowest price growth of any state with usable data.

Home value change, 2005 to latest year (highest and lowest 8 states)

Stop 8 — final stop

8 Puerto Rico's outflow spiked the year after Hurricane Maria

Hurricane Maria made landfall in September 2017. In the 2018 survey year that followed, 133,451 people left Puerto Rico for the mainland — up 37% from 97,488 the year before, and the highest outflow anywhere in the record.
